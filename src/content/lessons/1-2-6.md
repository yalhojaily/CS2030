Write mathematics in Markdown with `$...$` and `$$...$$` (GitHub renders it), or in LaTeX if you prefer and submit the PDF. For every problem, use this structure:

```markdown
### P7. Power sets and intersection
**Claim.** For all sets A and B, P(A) ∩ P(B) = P(A ∩ B).
```

The "Notes" line is not optional. Writing down where you struggled is how you will find your weak spots before the oral defense does.

## Part A — Proofs (12 problems)

Each proof is graded on the rubric at the end. Use the technique given in brackets unless you find a cleaner valid one, and say which you used.

<div class="exercise">

<span class="tag">P1</span><span class="lvl-tag">direct · ★★</span>

Prove that if $n$ is an odd integer, then $8 \mid n^2 - 1$.

*Hint:* write $n = 2k + 1$ and factor. Then use the fact that one of $k$ and $k + 1$ is even.

</div>

<div class="exercise">

<span class="tag">P2</span><span class="lvl-tag">contrapositive · ★</span>

Let $a$ and $b$ be integers. Prove that if $a + b \ge 19$, then $a \ge 10$ or $b \ge 10$.

</div>

<div class="exercise">

<span class="tag">P3</span><span class="lvl-tag">contradiction · ★★</span>

Prove that there are infinitely many primes (Euclid). You may use the result of lesson 1.2.3 that every integer $n \ge 2$ has a prime factor.

*Hint:* suppose $p_1, \dots, p_k$ are all the primes and consider $p_1 p_2 \cdots p_k + 1$.

</div>

<div class="exercise">

<span class="tag">P4</span><span class="lvl-tag">cases · ★★</span>

Prove that for every integer $n$, the remainder of $n^2$ on division by 4 is 0 or 1. Then prove, as a corollary, that no integer of the form $4k + 3$ is a sum of two squares.

</div>

<div class="exercise">

<span class="tag">P5</span><span class="lvl-tag">if and only if · ★★</span>

Prove that for every integer $n$: $6 \mid n$ if and only if $2 \mid n$ and $3 \mid n$.

*Hint:* the backward direction is the interesting one. If $n = 2a$ and $3 \mid n$, what can you say about $a$? (You may use: if $3 \mid 2a$ then $3 \mid a$; prove this too, by cases on $a$ mod 3.)

</div>

<div class="exercise">

<span class="tag">P6</span><span class="lvl-tag">sets · ★</span>

Prove that for all sets $A$ and $B$: $A \subseteq B$ if and only if $A \cup B = B$.

</div>

<div class="exercise">

<span class="tag">P7</span><span class="lvl-tag">sets · ★★</span>

Prove that $\mathcal{P}(A) \cap \mathcal{P}(B) = \mathcal{P}(A \cap B)$. Then decide whether $\mathcal{P}(A) \cup \mathcal{P}(B) = \mathcal{P}(A \cup B)$ always holds: prove it or give a counterexample.

</div>

<div class="exercise">

<span class="tag">P8</span><span class="lvl-tag">functions · ★★</span>

Let $f : A \to B$ and $g : B \to C$. Prove that if $g \circ f$ is injective, then $f$ is injective. Then give an explicit example where $g \circ f$ is injective but $g$ is not.

</div>

<div class="exercise">

<span class="tag">P9</span><span class="lvl-tag">relations · ★★</span>

On the set $\mathbb{Z} \times (\mathbb{Z} - \{0\})$ define $(a, b) \sim (c, d)$ if $ad = bc$. Prove that $\sim$ is an equivalence relation. Describe the equivalence class of $(1, 2)$ and explain in two sentences what these classes "are".

*Hint:* transitivity is the only step with real work. You will need to use that the second coordinates are non-zero.

</div>

<div class="exercise">

<span class="tag">P10</span><span class="lvl-tag">induction · ★★</span>

Prove that for every $n \ge 1$: $\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{n} i \cdot i! = (n+1)! - 1$.

</div>

<div class="exercise">

<span class="tag">P11</span><span class="lvl-tag">induction · ★★</span>

Find the smallest integer $b$ such that $2^n > n^2$ for **all** $n \ge b$, and prove the inequality for all $n \ge b$ by induction. (Check small cases first: it holds for $n = 1$, then fails for a while.)

</div>

<div class="exercise">

<span class="tag">P12</span><span class="lvl-tag">strong induction · ★★★</span>

Prove that every positive integer can be written as a sum of **distinct** powers of 2. (This is the existence half of binary representation.)

*Hint:* for $n + 1$, consider separately whether it is even or odd. If it is even, look at $(n+1)/2$.

</div>

<div class="exercise">

<span class="tag">P13 (bonus)</span><span class="lvl-tag">structural induction · ★★★</span>

Define the height of a binary tree as $-1$ for the empty tree and $1 + \max(h_L, h_R)$ for a node. Prove that a binary tree of height $h$ has at most $2^{h+1} - 1$ nodes. Deduce a lower bound on the height of a binary tree with $n$ nodes, and explain why this matters for search trees (lesson 1.3.5).

</div>

## Part C — Graphs (4 problems)

<div class="exercise">

<span class="tag">G1</span><span class="lvl-tag">proof · ★★★</span>

Prove that a simple graph with $n$ vertices and more than $\binom{n-1}{2}$ edges must be connected.

*Hint:* prove the contrapositive. If the graph is disconnected, split the vertices into one component of size $k$ and the rest, and bound the number of edges.

</div>

<div class="exercise">

<span class="tag">G2</span><span class="lvl-tag">proof · ★★</span>

Prove that a tree that has a vertex of degree $k$ has at least $k$ leaves.

*Hint:* either use the handshake lemma together with $|E| = n - 1$, or follow a path from each neighbour of the high-degree vertex.

</div>

<div class="exercise">

<span class="tag">G3</span><span class="lvl-tag">analysis · ★★</span>

Consider the cube graph $Q_3$: vertices are the bit strings of length 3, and two are adjacent if they differ in exactly one bit. (a) Does it have an Euler circuit or an Euler path? (b) Does it have a Hamilton cycle? If yes, write one down. (c) Is it bipartite? Justify each answer, then confirm (a) and (c) in the graph analyser from lesson 1.2.5.

</div>

<div class="exercise">

<span class="tag">G4</span><span class="lvl-tag">modelling · ★★</span>

Six exams, A to F, must be scheduled so that no student has two exams in the same slot. Students take these pairs together: A–B, A–C, B–C, B–D, C–E, D–E, D–F, E–F. Model this as a graph colouring problem. Find the minimum number of time slots, give a schedule that achieves it, and **prove** that fewer slots are impossible.

<details><summary>Check your final number</summary>

3 slots.

</details>
</div>

## Grading rubric

Grade yourself first, honestly, then compare with a peer or with your oral defense.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|:-|:-|:-|:-|
| **Correctness** | gap or false step | minor gap that is easy to fill | every step valid |
| **Structure** | technique unclear | technique stated, parts hard to find | technique stated; base case, IH and step (or each direction, or each case) clearly marked |
| **Definitions** | intuition instead of definitions | definitions used but not written out | definitions written in symbols and used |
| **Writing** | symbols without sentences | readable but loose | complete sentences, every variable introduced |

Each proof is out of 8. A proof with a 0 in correctness is redone, whatever the other scores. **Pass mark for the unit:** at least 75% overall, and no proof with a correctness score of 0 left unrepaired.
